**Causes and effects, as interpretations (not facts: i01–i04):**
- Specialization moved value to fabless designers, pure-play foundries and IP licensors, while IDMs shed fabs.
- Each wave of trade and policy action followed a perceived competitive or security threat: Japan in the 1980s, then China from 2014. The 2020–22 shortage added a supply-security reason for subsidies.
- Memory is the most cyclical segment.
- By 2024–25, AI data-center demand is clearly visible in primary data.

## 2. Participant types that emerged or disappeared (ontology proposals, with evidence)
- **Emerged:**
  - **Pure-play foundry** (UMC 1980, pure-play from 1995; TSMC 1987): c18–c20.
  - **Fabless design company** (1980s; trade body founded 1994): c22, c23.
  - **Semiconductor IP licensor** (Arm 1990): c24.
  - **Government/state investment fund as equity investor** (China's Big Fund 2014; US stake in Intel 2025): c44–c45, c56.
  - **Export-control authority** as a central actor (BIS, Dutch government): c48, c52, c53.
  - **Government-industry R&D consortium** (SEMATECH 1987): c16.
- **Transformed or declined:**
  - **Vertically integrated IDM**: AMD spun off its fabs (c26), UMC dropped its IDM model (c19), and Intel moved to a hybrid with a foundry business (c27).
  - **US merchant DRAM makers** almost disappeared after 1985 (c11, c12).
  - **Japanese DRAM makers** ended with Elpida's 2012 bankruptcy (c35).
- **Also needed, not evidenced in this pass:**
  - OSAT (outsourced assembly and test)
  - EDA vendors
  - Equipment makers (only ASML/EUV touched, c57)
  - Materials suppliers (Japan's share of photoresist and HF, c50)
  - Distributors
  - Hyperscalers that design their own chips
